Polish citizenship by descent with Jewish ancestry

Descendants of Polish Jews claim citizenship under exactly the same acts as everyone else; religion and ethnicity are not conditions. A few historical events, though, decide many of these cases, and the dates matter more than anything else.

The basic chain

Your ancestor must have been a Polish citizen after 31 January 1920 (registered in a commune of interwar Poland, holding no other citizenship), and every link down to you must have kept that citizenship. The checker and the rules page cover the general breaks: naturalisation before 19 January 1951, a father's loss extending to his minor children, descent through the father before 1951 for children born in wedlock.

Points that decide many Jewish-family cases

  • Emigration to Mandate Palestine or Israel before 19 January 1951. Taking Palestinian or Israeli citizenship before that date was acquiring a foreign citizenship, and it generally ended Polish citizenship, as it did in the US. Service in the IDF before that date can also count as foreign military service; the Polish embassy in Israel asks for IDF records in these cases.
  • Emigration from 19 January 1951 onwards. Taking Israeli or any other citizenship no longer ended Polish citizenship by itself. Survivors who were Polish citizens and left after that date usually kept it.
  • One-way travel documents, 1958–84 (including the 1968 emigration). The authorities treated these emigrants as having given up citizenship under an unpublished 1958 Council of State resolution. Administrative courts, including the Supreme Administrative Court, have ruled that emigrants kept their citizenship where no individual permission was issued, but regional offices still refuse some of these cases, so plan for a possible appeal.
  • Deprivation under the 1938 act. Citizens who had lived abroad for years could be stripped of citizenship by an individual decision; the loss extended to the wife and minor children. The archives show whether a decision was issued; without one, nothing was lost.

Records that help

  • Birth, marriage and death records from Polish civil registries (JRI-Poland indexes many of them).
  • A pre-war Polish passport, consular registration or military registration.
  • Emigration and arrival dates, and the date of any foreign naturalisation or immigration to Palestine/Israel.
  • For 1958–84 emigrants: the travel document and the emigration file.
